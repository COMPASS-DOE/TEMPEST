These data are measurements of soil gas well samples collected in 2021-2025 from the COMPASS Terrestrial Ecosystem Manipulation to Probe the Effects of Storm Treatments (TEMPEST) site in the Chesapeake Bay region. This manipulative, ecosystem-scale TEMPEST experiment addresses the potential for freshwater and estuarine-water disturbance events to alter tree function, species composition, and ecosystem processes in a deciduous coastal forest in MD, USA. The experiment uses a large-unit (2000 m2), un-replicated experimental design, with three 50 m × 40 m plots serving as control, freshwater, and saltwater treatments. The TEMPEST Project uses soil gas well nests to measure carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide concentrations in soil air at 5-10, 15-20, 30-35, and 45-50 cm below the soil surface. Gas samples were collected from gas wells semi-monthly from 2021-2025, and 1-2 times per day during flooding events. When water samples were collected from a gas well, the water was shaken with an equal volume of air and the air was then extracted for the gas sample. The samples were stored in exetainers and analyzed for methane, carbon dioxide, and nitrous oxide on a Varian 450 Gas Chromatograph (2021-2024) or a Shimazu Gas Chromatograph (2025). The methane is determined with an FID, the carbon dioxide with a TCD, and the nitrous oxide with an ECD. Analysis of each constituent is described in the data dictionary and further details, including quality assurance and quality control for all analyses can be found on the COMPASS TEMPEST page (https://github.com/COMPASS-DOE/TEMPEST)
